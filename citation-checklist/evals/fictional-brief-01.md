## I. Summary judgment standard

¶1 A movant for traditional summary judgment must show that no genuine issue of material fact exists and that it is entitled to judgment as a matter of law. Tex. R. Civ. P. 166a(c). The court takes as true all evidence favorable to the nonmovant. Harrow v. Linden Freight Co., 412 S.W.3d 118, 124 (Tex. App.—Austin 2013, pet. denied). As the Harrow court put it, "every reasonable inference must be indulged in favor of the nonmovant." Id. at 125.

¶2 Limitations is an affirmative defense, and the movant bears the burden to prove it conclusively. Ortega v. Pell Mercantile, Inc., 87 F.4th 901, 907 (5th Cir. 2023).

## II. The claim is timely

¶3 A two-year limitations period governs Whitlock's personal injury claim. Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code § 16.003(a). But the discovery rule defers accrual until the plaintiff knew or reasonably should have known of the injury. Quarry Hill Partners v. Vance, 233 S.W.3d 540, 546 (Tex. 2007). As the court explained, "accrual waits on knowledge, not on the calendar." Quarry Hill, 233 S.W.3d 540.

¶4 Courts apply the rule to latent injuries at storage facilities. See Harrow, 412 S.W.3d at 124; Ortega, 87 F.4th at 907. Id. at 908.

¶5 The Fifth Circuit reached the same result on similar facts. Teller v. Aurum Logistics, 99 F.4th 1022, 1030 (5th Cir. 2027).

## III. Greyhaven's authorities are distinguishable

¶6 Greyhaven relies on Belcourt Holdings, LLC v. Strand, 655 F. Supp. 3d 210, 208 (S.D. Tex. 2022), but that case involved an injury the plaintiff already knew about.

¶7 Greyhaven also cites Kessler, 301 S.W.3d at 77, for the proposition that storage operators owe no duty to inspect. See also Marsh v. Doyle Transfer, 512 S.W.3d (Tex. App.—Dallas 2017, no pet.).

¶8 Texas courts have applied the discovery rule where the injury was inherently undiscoverable. Harrow v. Linden Freight Co., 421 S.W.3d 118, 126 (Tex. App.—Austin 2013, pet. denied).
