# NDA clause types

The clause types an NDA playbook covers, in the order the NDA Playbook Builder asks about them. The NDA Review skill uses the same IDs to match an NDA's clauses to playbook positions. Keep this file identical in both skills.

Each entry gives:

- **Covers:** what the clause does.
- **Spot it by:** headings and phrases that usually signal it.
- **Ask:** the questions the builder asks, one at a time.
- **Common positions:** options the builder may offer as choices. They are common starting points, not advice. The attorney's answer is the position.

Core types come first. Optional types are asked only if the attorney wants a fuller playbook.

---

## Core

### nda-structure

- **Covers:** whether the NDA binds one party (one-way) or both (mutual).
- **Spot it by:** "mutual", "each party", "Disclosing Party" and "Receiving Party" defined for one side only.
- **Ask:** Which side do you usually represent? Do you insist on a mutual NDA, and if not, when is one-way acceptable?
- **Common positions:**
  - Mutual whenever our client will share anything.
  - One-way protecting the other side is fine when our client only receives.
  - One-way binding our client only if it is narrowed to marked information and the stated purpose.

### confidential-information-definition

- **Covers:** what counts as Confidential Information.
- **Spot it by:** "Confidential Information means", "whether or not marked", "oral", "visual".
- **Ask:** How broad should the definition be when your client receives? And when your client discloses?
- **Common positions:**
  - Marked as confidential, or reasonably understood to be confidential from its nature and the circumstances.
  - Marked, with oral disclosures identified as confidential in writing within 30 days.
  - Everything disclosed, whether or not marked (broadest; usually the discloser's ask).

### exclusions

- **Covers:** information that is not confidential even if disclosed.
- **Spot it by:** "shall not include", "does not apply to", "publicly available", "independently developed".
- **Ask:** Which exclusions must every NDA have? Is a missing exclusion a deal-breaker?
- **Common positions:** require all four standard exclusions:
  1. publicly available through no fault of the recipient;
  2. already known to the recipient without restriction;
  3. independently developed without use of the information;
  4. received from a third party free to disclose it.

### purpose-and-use

- **Covers:** what the recipient may use the information for.
- **Spot it by:** "Purpose", "solely for", "evaluating", "the Transaction".
- **Ask:** How specific must the purpose be? Any uses you never allow?
- **Common positions:** use only for a stated purpose; a defined, specific purpose rather than "business purposes".

### permitted-recipients

- **Covers:** who the recipient may share information with.
- **Spot it by:** "Representatives", "need to know", "employees", "advisors", "affiliates".
- **Ask:** Who must your client be able to share with? Who should the other side never share with?
- **Common positions:**
  - Employees, affiliates, legal and financial advisers, contractors and financing sources with a need to know, bound by confidentiality duties at least as protective.
  - Employees and advisers only.
  - The recipient is responsible for its recipients' breaches.

### standard-of-care

- **Covers:** how carefully the recipient must protect the information.
- **Spot it by:** "reasonable care", "same degree of care", "security measures".
- **Ask:** What standard of care do you accept? Any specific security duties you refuse?
- **Common positions:** reasonable care, and no less than the care used for one's own similar information; refuse named certifications or strict liability in an NDA.

### compelled-disclosure

- **Covers:** what happens if law, regulation or a court requires disclosure.
- **Spot it by:** "required by law", "subpoena", "court order", "protective order".
- **Ask:** What must the recipient do before a legally required disclosure?
- **Common positions:**
  - Prompt notice where legally permitted, disclosure limited to what is required, and reasonable help seeking protection at the discloser's cost.
  - Refuse a duty to obtain a protective order before any disclosure.

### term-and-survival

- **Covers:** how long the agreement lasts and how long the duties survive.
- **Spot it by:** "Term", "survive", "years from the date", "perpetual", "trade secrets".
- **Ask:** How long should confidentiality last for ordinary information? For trade secrets? What is the longest you accept?
- **Common positions:**
  - 2 years for ordinary information, 3 at most.
  - 5 years, or longer in regulated industries.
  - Trade secrets protected for as long as they remain trade secrets.

### return-or-destruction

- **Covers:** what happens to the information when the relationship ends.
- **Spot it by:** "return or destroy", "certify", "upon request", "copies".
- **Ask:** What exceptions does your client need when returning or destroying information?
- **Common positions:** return or destroy on request; certify on request; keep copies required by law or regulation and in routine backups, still subject to confidentiality.

### non-solicitation

- **Covers:** a promise not to hire or solicit the other side's employees or customers.
- **Spot it by:** "solicit", "hire", "employ", "induce", "customers".
- **Ask:** Do you ever accept a non-solicit in an NDA? If so, how narrow?
- **Common positions:**
  - Never in an NDA: delete it.
  - Only employees met through the evaluation, active solicitation only, general advertising excluded, 12 months or less, with attorney approval.

### non-competition-and-standstill

- **Covers:** a promise not to compete, or (in deals) not to buy the other side's securities.
- **Spot it by:** "compete", "standstill", "acquire securities", "exclusivity".
- **Ask:** Do you ever accept a non-compete or standstill in an NDA?
- **Common positions:** never a non-compete in an NDA; a standstill only in an M&A deal, with attorney approval.

### indemnity

- **Covers:** a promise to cover the other side's losses from a breach.
- **Spot it by:** "indemnify", "hold harmless", "defend".
- **Ask:** Do you ever accept an indemnity in an NDA?
- **Common positions:** never; damages and injunctive relief are enough.

### remedies

- **Covers:** what the discloser can do after a breach.
- **Spot it by:** "irreparable harm", "injunctive relief", "equitable relief", "bond", "liquidated damages", "attorneys' fees".
- **Ask:** What remedy terms do you accept? Which do you strike?
- **Common positions:**
  - Accept injunctive relief language.
  - Strike a waiver of bond, liquidated damages and one-way fee shifting.

### governing-law-and-forum

- **Covers:** which law applies and where disputes go.
- **Spot it by:** "governed by", "laws of the State of", "exclusive jurisdiction", "venue", "arbitration".
- **Ask:** Which governing law and courts do you require? What will you accept from the other side?
- **Common positions:**
  - Our client's home state.
  - Any US state, with courts in that state.
  - Escalate non-US law or arbitration.

## Optional

### residuals

- **Covers:** a right to use information retained in unaided memory.
- **Spot it by:** "residuals", "unaided memory", "retained in the minds".
- **Ask:** Do you accept a residuals clause when your client discloses? When it receives?
- **Common positions:** none when our client discloses; when our client receives, ask for one only if its engineers will see technical information, and escalate.

### no-license-and-no-warranty

- **Covers:** that disclosure grants no rights and makes no promises about accuracy.
- **Spot it by:** "no license", "as is", "no representation or warranty".
- **Ask:** Must every NDA say that disclosure grants no license and gives no warranty?
- **Common positions:** both should be present; flag if missing, no deal-breaker.

### assignment

- **Covers:** whether a party may transfer the NDA.
- **Spot it by:** "assign", "transfer", "successors", "change of control".
- **Ask:** What assignment terms do you require?
- **Common positions:** mutual consent, not unreasonably withheld, with free assignment to affiliates and to a successor in a merger or sale.

### publicity-and-no-obligation

- **Covers:** no announcements, and no duty to do a deal.
- **Spot it by:** "publicity", "announcement", "no obligation to proceed", "definitive agreement".
- **Ask:** Do you require a no-publicity clause and a statement that neither side must proceed?
- **Common positions:** both should be present; flag if missing.

### whistleblower-immunity-notice

- **Covers:** the notice of immunity under the Defend Trade Secrets Act, 18 U.S.C. § 1833(b), in agreements with an individual employee, contractor or consultant.
- **Spot it by:** "Defend Trade Secrets Act", "18 U.S.C. § 1833(b)", "immunity", "whistleblower".
- **Ask:** When your client is the employer and the other party is an individual, do you require the notice?
- **Common positions:** include it whenever the other party is an individual employee, contractor or consultant. Without it, the employer can't recover exemplary damages or attorney fees under the Act from that individual.

### personal-data

- **Covers:** personal information about people, if the NDA covers it.
- **Spot it by:** "personal data", "personal information", "privacy", "data protection".
- **Ask:** What do you do when an NDA will cover personal data?
- **Common positions:** escalate; personal data usually needs data protection terms an NDA doesn't provide.

### general-provisions

- **Covers:** entire agreement, amendments, notices, severability, waiver and counterparts.
- **Spot it by:** "entire agreement", "amended only", "notices", "severability", "waiver", "counterparts".
- **Ask:** Is there any general provision you insist on or refuse?
- **Common positions:** standard provisions that apply to both sides are acceptable as is; flag any that give only one side a right, such as a right to amend alone.

---

## other

Any clause that fits none of the types above. The NDA Review skill reports it as **not covered by the playbook** and asks the attorney to review it.
