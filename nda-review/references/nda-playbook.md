# NDA Playbook: Fictional startup practice (example)

Version 1.0 · 2026-11-17 · Built with the NDA Playbook Builder (Code + Counsel). A licensed attorney must review this playbook before anyone relies on it.

> **Fictional example.** This playbook belongs to no real firm, attorney or client. It shows the format and level of detail. Replace it with your own positions.

**Applies to:** NDAs where we act for either side.
**Our usual clients:** early-stage technology companies sharing product and financial information with partners, vendors and investors.

## How to read this playbook

- **Must fix:** a red line. The NDA can't be signed with this term.
- **Should fix:** outside our fallbacks. Negotiate it.
- **Note:** within a fallback. Flag it; no change needed.
- **No position:** we haven't set one. Escalate to the attorney.

## Global rules

- Obligations bind both sides unless our client only receives information.
- An NDA covers confidentiality only. Hiring limits, competition limits, exclusivity and indemnities belong in other agreements.
- When a clause isn't covered here, escalate to the attorney.

## Positions

### nda-structure · NDA structure

- **Applies when:** either side
- **Preferred:** mutual whenever our client will share any information.
- **Fallbacks, in order:**
  1. One-way protecting the other side, when our client only receives.
  2. One-way binding only our client, narrowed to information marked confidential and to the stated Purpose.
- **Red line:** None.
- **If the NDA is silent:** not applicable.
- **Why:** our clients usually share as much as they receive, and a one-way NDA leaves their information unprotected.
- **Source:** interview

### confidential-information-definition · Definition of Confidential Information

- **Applies when:** our client receives. When our client discloses, a broad definition is fine.
- **Preferred:** information marked confidential, or reasonably understood to be confidential from its nature and the circumstances of disclosure.
- **Fallbacks, in order:**
  1. Marked information, with oral disclosures identified as confidential in writing within 30 days.
- **Red line:** None.
- **If the NDA is silent:** Should fix.
- **Why:** a definition covering everything "whether or not marked" makes every conversation confidential, and our clients can't track that.
- **Source:** interview

### exclusions · Exclusions

- **Applies when:** either side
- **Preferred:** all four standard exclusions: publicly available through no fault of the recipient; already known without restriction; independently developed without use of the information; received from a third party free to disclose it.
- **Fallbacks, in order:** None.
- **Red line:** when our client receives, a missing independent-development or third-party exclusion.
- **If the NDA is silent:** Must fix when our client receives; Should fix when it discloses.
- **Why:** without those exclusions, our client can be accused of misusing work it did itself or information it got elsewhere.
- **Source:** interview

### purpose-and-use · Purpose and use

- **Applies when:** our client discloses
- **Preferred:** the recipient may use the information only for a defined, specific Purpose.
- **Fallbacks, in order:**
  1. A general "evaluating a business relationship" purpose, when the information is not technical.
- **Red line:** None.
- **If the NDA is silent:** Should fix.
- **Why:** a use limit is what makes misuse a breach.
- **Source:** interview

### permitted-recipients · Permitted recipients

- **Applies when:** our client receives
- **Preferred:** employees, affiliates, legal and financial advisers, contractors and financing sources with a need to know, bound by confidentiality duties at least as protective as the NDA's.
- **Fallbacks, in order:**
  1. Employees and legal and financial advisers only.
- **Red line:** None.
- **If the NDA is silent:** Should fix.
- **Why:** our clients rely on outside advisers and contractors to evaluate deals.
- **Source:** interview

### standard-of-care · Standard of care

- **Applies when:** our client receives
- **Preferred:** reasonable care, and no less than the care our client uses for its own similar information.
- **Fallbacks, in order:** None.
- **Red line:** strict liability for any disclosure, or a duty to hold named security certifications.
- **If the NDA is silent:** acceptable as is.
- **Why:** an NDA shouldn't carry a security program our clients can't run.
- **Source:** interview

### compelled-disclosure · Compelled disclosure

- **Applies when:** our client receives
- **Preferred:** prompt notice where legally permitted, disclosure limited to what is legally required, and reasonable help seeking protection at the discloser's cost.
- **Fallbacks, in order:**
  1. The same, with our client bearing its own minor costs.
- **Red line:** None. A duty to obtain a protective order before any disclosure is Should fix.
- **If the NDA is silent:** Should fix.
- **Why:** our client must be able to obey a subpoena without breaching the NDA.
- **Our wording:** "If the Recipient is required by law, regulation or court order to disclose Confidential Information, it may do so after giving the Discloser prompt notice where legally permitted, and only to the extent required."
- **Source:** template clause 6, quoted above

### term-and-survival · Term and survival

- **Applies when:** our client receives. When our client discloses, longer protection is fine.
- **Preferred:** confidentiality lasts 2 years after the last disclosure; trade secrets stay protected for as long as they remain trade secrets.
- **Fallbacks, in order:**
  1. 3 years after the last disclosure.
- **Red line:** longer than 3 years, or perpetual, for ordinary information.
- **If the NDA is silent:** Should fix.
- **Why:** our clients can't track obligations indefinitely, and 3 years covers the deal cycles we see.
- **Source:** interview

### return-or-destruction · Return or destruction

- **Applies when:** our client receives
- **Preferred:** return or destroy on request and certify on request, keeping copies required by law or regulation and copies in routine backups, which stay confidential.
- **Fallbacks, in order:**
  1. Certification only on written request.
- **Red line:** None. No exception for legal retention or backups is Should fix.
- **If the NDA is silent:** Note.
- **Why:** backups can't be destroyed one file at a time.
- **Source:** interview

### non-solicitation · Non-solicitation

- **Applies when:** either side
- **Preferred:** no non-solicitation clause.
- **Fallbacks, in order:** None.
- **Red line:** any promise not to solicit or hire employees, contractors or customers.
- **If the NDA is silent:** acceptable as is.
- **Why:** hiring limits are business terms, not confidentiality terms.
- **Source:** interview

### non-competition-and-standstill · Non-competition and standstill

- **Applies when:** either side
- **Preferred:** no non-compete, exclusivity or standstill.
- **Fallbacks, in order:** None.
- **Red line:** any non-compete or exclusivity. A standstill goes under Open questions.
- **If the NDA is silent:** acceptable as is.
- **Why:** an NDA can't restrict our client's business.
- **Source:** interview

### indemnity · Indemnity

- **Applies when:** either side
- **Preferred:** no indemnity.
- **Fallbacks, in order:** None.
- **Red line:** any duty to indemnify, defend or hold harmless for a breach.
- **If the NDA is silent:** acceptable as is.
- **Why:** damages and injunctive relief are enough remedy for an NDA breach.
- **Source:** suggested, accepted

### remedies · Remedies

- **Applies when:** either side
- **Preferred:** injunctive relief language is acceptable.
- **Fallbacks, in order:** None.
- **Red line:** None. A waiver of bond, liquidated damages or one-way attorney fees is Should fix.
- **If the NDA is silent:** acceptable as is.
- **Why:** courts decide bond and fees; the NDA shouldn't tilt them.
- **Source:** interview

### governing-law-and-forum · Governing law and forum

- **Applies when:** either side
- **Preferred:** the law and courts of our client's home state.
- **Fallbacks, in order:**
  1. The law and courts of any US state.
- **Red line:** None. Non-US law or courts is Should fix; arbitration goes to the attorney.
- **If the NDA is silent:** Note.
- **Why:** our clients can't fund disputes far from home.
- **Source:** interview

### residuals · Residuals

- **Applies when:** our client discloses
- **Preferred:** no residuals clause.
- **Fallbacks, in order:** None.
- **Red line:** None. Any residuals clause when our client discloses is Should fix.
- **If the NDA is silent:** acceptable as is.
- **Why:** a residuals clause lets the recipient use what it remembers, which defeats the NDA for technical information.
- **Source:** interview

### no-license-and-no-warranty · No license and no warranty

- **Applies when:** either side
- **Preferred:** the NDA says disclosure grants no license and information is provided as is.
- **Fallbacks, in order:** None.
- **Red line:** None.
- **If the NDA is silent:** Note.
- **Why:** it keeps ownership clear and avoids accuracy claims.
- **Source:** suggested, accepted

### assignment · Assignment

- **Applies when:** either side
- **Preferred:** no assignment without consent, not unreasonably withheld, except to affiliates or a successor in a merger or sale.
- **Fallbacks, in order:**
  1. No assignment without consent.
- **Red line:** None.
- **If the NDA is silent:** Note.
- **Why:** our clients get acquired, and the NDA should follow the business.
- **Source:** interview

### publicity-and-no-obligation · Publicity and no obligation

- **Applies when:** either side
- **Preferred:** no announcements without consent, and neither side must proceed with any deal.
- **Fallbacks, in order:** None.
- **Red line:** None.
- **If the NDA is silent:** Note.
- **Why:** an NDA shouldn't imply a deal.
- **Source:** suggested, accepted

### whistleblower-immunity-notice · Whistleblower immunity notice

- **Applies when:** our client is the employer and the other party is an individual employee, contractor or consultant.
- **Preferred:** include the immunity notice under 18 U.S.C. § 1833(b).
- **Fallbacks, in order:** None.
- **Red line:** None.
- **If the NDA is silent:** Should fix.
- **Why:** without the notice, our client can't recover exemplary damages or attorney fees under the Defend Trade Secrets Act from that individual.
- **Source:** interview

### general-provisions · General provisions

- **Applies when:** either side
- **Preferred:** standard provisions (entire agreement, amendment in a signed writing, notices, severability, waiver, counterparts) that apply equally to both sides.
- **Fallbacks, in order:** None.
- **Red line:** None. A provision that gives only one side a right, such as a right to amend alone, is Should fix.
- **If the NDA is silent:** acceptable as is.
- **Why:** boilerplate rarely matters until it is one-sided.
- **Source:** suggested, accepted
